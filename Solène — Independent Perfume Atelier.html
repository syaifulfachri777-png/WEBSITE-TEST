Every bottle unfolds in order

Top, heart, and base notes aren't a marketing device here — it's the actual chemistry of how a scent reveals itself over the day.

First — 0 to 15 min

Top note

Bergamot, pink pepper, and citrus peel. Bright and immediate, this is what greets a room before you speak.

Then — 1 to 4 hrs

Heart note

Iris, dried fig, and cedar. The true character of the fragrance, once the opening has settled into skin.

Finally — 4+ hrs

Base note

Amber, oakmoss, and vetiver. What remains on a scarf the next morning — the part people remember.

Why the colors are never decoration here

Every accent in our bottles and labels is chosen the same way we choose ingredients — for what it does to the person holding it, not what looks good in a photo.

Amber signals warmth before the wearer says a word. Mauve carries the same emotional register as rose absolute — intimacy, not sweetness. Sage settles a room the way vetiver settles a composition. Ink holds the others quiet, the way a good base note holds a fragrance together.
